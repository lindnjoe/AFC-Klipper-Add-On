Your Bambu AMS, running on your Klipper printer.

A small board that takes the Bambu printer's place on the AMS's own wiring. The AMS keeps its stock firmware, never gets opened, and behaves exactly as it always has — it just answers to Klipper now.

The problem

An AMS only talks to a Bambu printer.

Bambu's filament changer is good hardware — four bays, tag reading, a spool dryer, a buffer that manages slack. But it speaks a private language over its own cable, and it waits for a Bambu machine to lead the conversation. Plug it into anything else and it sits there doing nothing.

So if you run Klipper and the AFC add-on — on a Voron, a Trident, something you built yourself, or a commercial machine that supports it like the Snapmaker U1 — an AMS on the shelf is a box you can't use. The usual answer is to replace its brain with aftermarket boards and custom firmware. That works, and it also means taking a working machine apart.

What BridgeBox does

It stands where the printer would stand.

BridgeBox is the other half of that conversation. It plugs into the four-pin socket on the filament buffer — the one a Bambu printer normally occupies — and speaks the AMS's language back to it, on time, at full speed. From the AMS's side nothing has changed. It is still talking to a printer. That printer is now yours.

And none of it is one-way. No board is replaced, no firmware is flashed, no case is opened — so the day you want the AMS back on a Bambu machine, you unplug BridgeBox and plug the printer in. It will not know it was ever gone.

What you get

Everything the AMS already knew how to do.

Filament changes

Your slicer just changes tools

Multi-colour and multi-material prints work the way they do on a Bambu machine. Load, unload, the buffer holding tension — all of it driven from Klipper.

Spool tags

It reads the RFID tag

Put a tagged spool in a bay and the material, colour and temperatures arrive by themselves. No typing in what you just loaded.

Remaining filament

It measures what's left

The AMS turns the spool and measures it — a real figure, not a running total that drifts. It lands in Spoolman and on the spool cards in your web interface.

Drying

Dry a spool where it sits

On the units with heaters, start and stop a drying cycle from the printer's interface and watch the chamber temperature and humidity while it runs.

When it goes wrong

It notices, and it retries

A jam or a failed feed is caught, named in plain words, and retried. If it still can't recover it stops the print rather than carrying on with no filament.

Knock the cable out mid-print and it pauses within seconds — before the extruder can grind away at an AMS nobody is driving. A load already running rides out a brief blip and finishes.

More than one unit

Four boxed units and eight HTs

Chain up to four AMS 1 or AMS 2 Pro alongside eight HTs, mixed however you like — twenty-four bays on one bridge. That ceiling is Bambu's own, not ours: it is the room their bus leaves for addresses. The units claim theirs at power-up, exactly as they would behind a Bambu printer.

Before you buy

What you need, and what this isn't.

A Klipper printer running AFC

BridgeBox is a unit inside the Automated Filament Control (AFC) Klipper Add-on. If you already run AFC with a BoxTurtle or similar, an AMS becomes another thing it can drive.

A 24 V 2–4 A supply

The AMS was powered by the printer; now it needs its own — the one thing not in the box. Any 24 V adapter from 2 to 4 A does it: nothing in here draws near that, and a 2 A fuse on the board holds the ceiling whatever you plug in. The barrel matches an AMS 2 Pro's own adapter, so a spare one goes straight in. Buying rather than raiding a drawer? Get two — the heater runs on a supply of its own, and that is what the second is for.

The dryers need their own power

Heaters are not on this rail and shouldn't be. Two units drying at once would run the connector past its rating, and no amount of copper fixes a connector.

One config section, then swap units freely

This is a maker's product, not an appliance — you do edit your Klipper config. Once, though: one section, and it writes every unit, bay and sensor for you. After that the hardware is yours to rearrange. Plug an AMS in or pull one out while everything is running and it claims its own bays there and then — spare bays are held open for exactly that, so the ones you already had never shift and your Spoolman spools and tool numbers stay put.

Optional

A panel for the shelf.

A 5-inch touchscreen that shows what every bay is holding, what's loaded, and how a drying cycle is going — on the bench, without opening a laptop.

This one is firmware, not hardware. It is an image you flash onto a BTT Panda Touch or K-Touch — the same board under two names, sold as a Bambu accessory — which turns it into a display for your printer instead. If one is already sitting in a drawer since you stopped using it, that is the whole cost of this.

Tap a bay to scan its tag. Tap the dryer to start a cycle and watch the chamber temperature and the time left. It dims itself when it has been left alone, and a tap brings it straight back.

It reaches the printer over your WiFi and never touches the filament bus. Unplug it in the middle of a print and the print doesn't notice — nothing on the shelf should be able to spoil a job.
